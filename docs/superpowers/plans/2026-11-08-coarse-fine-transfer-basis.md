# 2D Coarse–Fine Transfer Basis Qualification

> Execute the tasks in order with fresh implementers and independent reviews. Preserve RED, GREEN, measurements, and decisions in ignored evidence.

**Goal.** Measure a bounded 2:1 MAC transfer basis candidate and expose any knot nesting, affine reproduction, support, or flux ownership failure before production adaptivity.

**Design authority.** [FluidiTy design](../specs/2026-11-07-fluidity-design.md), sections 4.2, 5, 7.3, 13.1 and 15.2. This spike uses an experimental Python/NumPy module. It leaves production CUDA, AMR storage, particle remap, solver integration, and 3D junction qualification to later work.

**Candidate.** Two uniform tensor-product levels with coarse width h and fine width h/2. One aligned half-plane begins at local coordinate zero on the interface axis. Each MAC component uses a degree-3 centered spline on its face-normal axis at integer face coordinates and degree-2 centered splines at half-offset transverse coordinates. For an axis of degree p and shift s, the child anchor index is 2i+s+j-(p+1)/2, with j=0…p+1 and refinement weight 2^(-p) times binomial(p+1,j). Use the tensor product of these masks. Active fine functions have entire closed support in the fine half-plane. Retain coarse parents outside it and truncate the child terms whose full support lies inside. Evaluate the retained child sum directly to avoid cancellation. Associate every basis function with its original level’s Greville anchor. For MAC quadratic-normal baseline, coarse knots at half-integer h do not lie on the dyadic fine normal knot lattice. Treat that measured/analytic obstruction as a result. Do not shift physical face locations to hide it.

**Terminology.** A face lattice anchor is a Greville position used for a transfer coefficient. A particle world position is a query. The refinement interface axis identifies the coarse/fine boundary and may differ from the face-normal axis. A coarse face flux equals the sum of its owned fine subface fluxes. Numerical support, physical cells, and face ownership are distinct.

Axes are 0 or 1. Accept finite 2D queries on bounded normalized patches with spacing positive and usable. Keep FP32 weights, gradients and reductions genuinely FP32. Use equivalent FP64 tests as precision cross-check.

Check each component on both orientations, including a 90-degree rotation with sign on the transverse coordinate, translated negative coordinates, spacing 0.5 and 2, and points on either side of and exactly at the interface. Physical gradients sum to zero and reproduce identity after nondimensionalization (FP32 <=2e-6, FP64 <=1e-12). Verify coarse-only, mixed and fine-only support far from and near the interface; include a knot and a fractional off-knot sample.
