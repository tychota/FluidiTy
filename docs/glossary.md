# FluidiTy glossary

This file is the source of truth for the words of the code base. A domain word may appear in an identifier or
a comment only if it is listed here (`AGENTS.md`, naming rule 2). The rules for using these words are in
`AGENTS.md`.

How to read the tables:

- **Say** is the word or name to use. `...` stands for the rest of a name.
- **Plain meaning** explains it for a good C++ programmer who has never read a fluid simulation paper. Units
  come first where the word is a physical quantity.
- **Never say** lists the words that are banned for that concept, separated by commas. A word in parentheses
  limits the ban to one sense: `component (for this)` bans `component` only for this concept, and `row (alone)`
  bans `row` unless it is part of the allowed phrase. A word without parentheses is banned everywhere. A banned word
  covers its regular inflections too: a banned verb its -s, -ed and -ing forms, a banned noun its plural; the rules
  check lists the few inflected forms that are words of their own (`WORDS_OF_THEIR_OWN` in `tools/check_rules.py`).
  The rules check prints a note, not a failure, for a word banned in one sense.
- A tool reads this file. Every vocabulary table keeps exactly the header `| Say | Plain meaning | Never say |`.

A new word is added here in the same commit that first uses it. Words that are still being settled are not
listed; they arrive with the module that settles them (`docs/design/programme.md`, section 9).

## Grid and geometry

| Say | Plain meaning | Never say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `StaggeredGrid`, `grid` | Pressure at cell centers, each velocity part on the faces it crosses. The header comment gives "also called MAC grid, Harlow and Welch 1965" once. | MAC, mac_grid |
| `NearbyFaceWeights`, `FaceWeight` | The grid faces near one particle, each with the weight the particle gives it. | stencil, support (for the grid faces near a particle), node (for a grid point near a particle), sample (for this), slot, row (alone) |
| `weight_curve`, `weight curve`, `WeightCurveValue`, `ComputeWeightCurve`, `kWeightCurveReach` | The function that turns a distance in cell widths into a weight, and its slope. Papers call it the quadratic B-spline. It is never negative and is zero, with zero slope, at and beyond `kWeightCurveReach` (1.5 cell widths). `kWeightCurveReach` is the curve's own name for where it ends; it is not the `reach` of a solid or the `particle reach` defined below. | basis, kernel (for this), quadratic kernel |
| `weighted spread`, `ComputeCompleteWeightedSpread` | m2. The symmetric 3 x 3 matrix S of `<eq:weighted-spread>`: the sum over the kept faces of the scaled weight times the face's offset from the weighted mean offset, multiplied by itself. `ComputeCompleteWeightedSpread` gives its diagonal entry when all 27 same-size faces are kept: the cell width squared over 4. | covariance, second moment |
| `kMaxNearbyFaces`, `kMinWeightSum`, `kMinPivotShare` | The most faces one axis lists for one position (216: no two candidate faces share one of the 6 x 6 x 6 half-width places of the window), the least sum of kept weights that still allows a fit (0.1), and the share of the trace at or below which a pivot of the fit is too small (`1e-12`). The fit factors the weighted spread divided by the cell width squared, and the trace is that matrix's own. | |
| `index` | Position in any array or sequence. | slot, ordinal |
| `place`, `CellPlaceIndex`, `FacePlaceIndex`, `CornerPlaceIndex` | A position in the arrays of one grid version (`grid/grid_ids.h`). It is valid for that version only: after the grid changes, the same cell may sit at another place. A cell block has 64 cell places, 240 face places and 125 corner places: cell place (x, y, z) of block b is `64 b + (4 z + y) * 4 + x`, corner place (x, y, z) is `125 b + (5 z + y) * 5 + x`. Use `id` for an identity that must survive the change. | slot |
| `id` | Stable identity that survives reordering. | |
| `cell_coord`, `CellCoord` | Integer (x, y, z) location of a cell. | CellIndex3D |
| `axis` | The x, y or z part of a vector or grid. | component (for this) |
| `cell_low`, `cell_high` | The two cells on the low and high side of a face, along that face's axis. | tail (for a cell on one side of a face), head (for a cell on one side of a face) |
| `side_left` (-X), `side_right` (+X), `side_front` (-Y), `side_back` (+Y), `side_bottom` (-Z), `side_top` (+Z) | Blender viewport names. Z is up. | low_x wall, floor (for a side: math::Floor keeps the word), upper (for a side of the domain), lower (for a side of the domain) |
| `into_...`, `out_of_...` | Direction of a flow or normal, always naming the thing: `flow_into_liquid`, `normal_out_of_solid`. | inward (without the thing), outward (without the thing), outward_normal |
| `touching` | Shares a boundary with. | incident |
| `partly_filled_cell` | A cell crossed by a solid or by the liquid surface. | cut cell |
| `open_area` | m2. Area of a face not blocked by solid. | |
| `open_fraction` | No unit, 0 to 1. The open area as a fraction of the face area. | aperture |
| `liquid_covered_area` | m2. Area of a solid face that liquid touches. | wetted |
| `cell_eighth` | One of the 8 sub-boxes of a cell, numbered `x + 2 y + 4 z`, 0..7, where each of x, y and z is 0 for the low half and 1 for the high half (`ComputeSmallCell`, `ComputeCellEighth`). | octant |
| `distance_to_surface` | m. Distance to the liquid surface, negative inside the liquid. | signed distance |
| `sample_weight` | Weight of one sample point in a sum that approximates an integral. | quadrature weight |

## Solids and the domain

| Say | Plain meaning | Never say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `solid`, `Solid`, `SolidShape` | Anything the liquid cannot enter. `Solid` is what the contact code reads: a shape, its pose at the start of the step and its motion during the step. | wall (for a whole solid), obstacle, body (for a solid) |
| `DomainBox`, `domain box`, `domain_side_...`, `DomainSide`, `ClosedSides` | The box the liquid lives in, open at the top by default. It is fixed and aligned with the world axes. Its faces are domain sides; a closed side stops particles. | tank, wall (for a domain side) |
| `FlatSolid`, `flat solid` | Fills one side of a plane. | halfspace |
| `Plate`, `plate` | Thin solid with two faces, unbounded sideways. | slab, blade |
| `Box`, `box` | Solid with six flat faces. | |
| `Sphere`, `sphere` | Round solid: every point within `radius` of its centre. | |
| `Cylinder`, `cylinder` | Finite solid cylinder: a round `wall` around its axis and two flat `cap`s. | |
| `wall` (of a cylinder) | The round surface of a cylinder, around its axis. Face `kCurved`. Only for a cylinder: a whole solid or a domain side is never a wall. | |
| `cap` | One of the two flat ends of a cylinder. Faces `kLowZ` and `kHighZ`. | |
| `rim` | The circle where a cylinder's wall meets a cap. Faces `kLowRim` and `kHighRim`. | |
| `rim corner` | The part of a cylinder grown by the contact tolerance with sharp edges that lies farther than one tolerance from the rim. A sweep that enters the grown cylinder there goes on by the rim search. | |
| `rim search`, `kRimHitGapShare`, `kMaxRimAdvanceCount` | How a sweep finds its first hit beside a rim of a cylinder that does not turn: from where the path enters the cylinder grown with sharp edges, advances of the gap divided by the speed at which it closes (the second-order advance of a solid that does not turn), on the true distance. A hit once the gap is at most `kRimHitGapShare` (2^-10) of the tolerance; free when the gap stops closing or would not close within the sweep; after `kMaxRimAdvanceCount` (16) advances a hit where it stands. | |
| `bounding surface`, `kBoundingSurfaceRestingDepthShare`, `kBoundingSurfaceSinkShare` | A surface that a whole convex solid lies behind: a face plane of a box, a cylinder's wall taken as unbounded along its axis, the plane of a cap. A sweep whose start rests on one that does not give its distance (a gap between minus an eighth and plus a sixteenth of the contact tolerance, sinking at most a sixty-fourth of it during the sweep; for a cylinder, only beside its rim) is free of the solid: `ComputeIsRestingOnBoundingSurface`. It is what lets a particle slide across the seam of two flush solids; see also `level with`. A particle on the last eighth of a tolerance of a box's grown top rests on the side plane and falls past the box, a known choice. | |
| `degenerate normal` | The normal chosen where several directions are equally near the surface: at a sphere's centre, and on a cylinder's axis where the wall is at least as near as the caps. It is minus the solid's own x axis, the normal of `kLowX`, as a box's tie gives. | |
| `spin` | A turn about a sphere's centre, a cylinder's axis, or the normal of a flat solid or a plate (both are unbounded sideways). It moves the surface but not the shape, so it does not count toward the turn limit of a step and does not make the solid a turning solid; friction still feels it through the solid velocity. | |
| `plate side`, `PlateSide`, `SolidSide` | One of the two sides of one plate, as a stable identity: the plate's id and the side. Particles on different plate sides never exchange values. | side_tag |
| `side of a plate`, `kAlongNormal`, `kAgainstNormal` | Where a point is relative to a plate: beyond the face its normal points to (`kAlongNormal`), or beyond the other face (`kAgainstNormal`). A point inside the thickness, or near a solid that is not a plate, is on no side (`kNoPlate`). `FindSolidSide` gives it. | |
| `solid face`, `SolidFace` | One part of a solid's surface, named in the solid's own axes: `kLowX` to `kHighZ` for flat faces, `kCurved`, `kLowRim`, `kHighRim` for round ones. | |
| `pose`, `SolidPose` | Where a solid is: the origin of its own axes, m, and the rotation whose columns are those axes in the world. | |
| `own axes`, `own point` | A solid's own x, y and z axes, in which its shape is described; a point written in them, from the solid's origin, is its own point (`Transpose(axes) * (x - origin)`). | |
| `face plane` | The plane of one flat face, taken as unbounded: of a flat solid, a plate, a box or a side of the domain box. | |
| `grown solid`, grown by the tolerance | A solid enlarged by one contact tolerance in every direction: a sweep's hit is where the path reaches it. A box, a plate or a cylinder grown with sharp edges is enlarged face plane by face plane, so its edges and corners stay sharp. | |
| `upright` (in examples and tests) | A flat solid whose normal is horizontal, standing on a floor. | |
| `seam`, `flush` | Two solids are flush when a face of one continues a face of the other with no step; the seam is the line where they meet. A particle sliding across a seam rests on the next solid's bounding surface (`bounding surface`). | |
| `motion`, `SolidMotion` | How a solid moves during one step: the linear velocity of its origin, m/s, and its angular velocity about the origin, rad/s. Constant during the step. | |
| `fixed`, `driven`, `free` | Motion: never moves; follows a given path; pushed by the liquid. Combine with a shape: `DrivenFlatSolid`, `DrivenPlate`, `FreeBox`. | prescribed, rigid body, floating (for motion) |
| `wave_paddle` (a variable, not a type) | The driven flat solid that makes waves. It is its own solid, not part of the domain box. | piston |
| `solid_velocity`, `SolidVelocitySample` | m/s. Velocity of a solid's surface. | wall_velocity, LiquidPrescribedWallSample3D |
| `nearest solid surface` | The surface, among all solids, with the smallest distance from a point; equal distances keep the solid that comes first. `ComputeSolidVelocitySample` gives it, with its velocity, when it lies within the search distance. | |
| `search distance` | m. The largest distance at which `ComputeSolidVelocitySample` looks for the nearest solid surface; farther surfaces give no solid. Its parameter is `search_distance`. | reach (for this) |
| `same_side` | Both are on the same side of a plate, so they may exchange data. | compatible (for two things on the same side of a plate) |
| `elapsed` | Clock time: `work_elapsed_seconds`. In contact and solids, seconds since the start of the step: `ComputeSolidPose(solid, elapsed)`, `start_elapsed`. | wall (for time) |
| `contact tolerance`, `contact_tolerance` | m. The distance from a solid's surface at which a particle rests, a kept distance, not an allowed error: `2^-30` of the smallest cell width plus `2^-40` of the largest coordinate a solid reaches during the step, far above the rounding of a computed distance. A solid or a cell narrower than 1024 contact tolerances fails `CheckContactTolerance` (`kSolidTooThin`); the tolerance is never made larger to let contact pass. | certified, nudge, penetration tolerance, resolution (for this) |
| `ContactScale` | The lengths of a scene that set its contact tolerance: the largest coordinate a solid reaches during the step, the smallest cell width and the thinnest solid. `ComputeContactScale` gives it. | |
| `reach` (of a solid) | m. `Length(half_size)` of a box, a domain box, a sphere or a cylinder: no point of the solid is farther from its origin. Exact for a box (its corners); an upper bound for a sphere (`Sqrt(3)` times the radius) and a cylinder. A flat solid and a plate have none. Not the `particle reach`. | |
| `rest gap` | m. The one contact tolerance a particle in contact keeps between itself and a solid's surface: it rests at distance `contact_tolerance`, never at 0, so any later evaluation of the distance is positive. | nudge |
| `close call`, `ContactCloseCall` | A step that contact cannot answer safely at this step length. Some close calls are cured by a shorter step and some are not; `can_shorter_step_cure` says which. | near miss, edge case (for this) |
| `can_shorter_step_cure` | Of a `ContactFailure`: a shorter step may cure it. True only for a close call met at a hit after the start of the step, or whose test reads the time left; false for every other error and for a close call of a hit at the start that reads no time left (a box driven down onto the floor, two reacting solids pushed by the start's response, a turning solid resting against another), which recurs at every step length. True says a cure exists, not that one halving finds it. | |
| `sweep` | The straight path of a particle during all or part of a step, `start + velocity_for_this_step * t` for `0 <= t <= duration`, tested against the solids grown by the contact tolerance. `FindFirstHit` finds the first hit of one sweep. | |
| `gap` (to a solid) | m. A particle's distance to a solid, or to one of its surfaces, minus the contact tolerance: zero at a hit, negative inside the solid grown by the tolerance. To a face plane of a solid that does not turn it changes linearly with time along a sweep; to a sphere or a cylinder it does not. Not the `rest gap`, which is the distance kept at rest (one contact tolerance): at rest the gap is 0. Not a `narrow gap` either, which is a space between solids. A distance not reduced by the tolerance is called a distance, never a gap. | |
| `hit`, `FirstHit`, `hit_count` | A moment in a step, along a particle's path or at its start, at which both its velocities get the joint response over every surface it is on, whatever their number: a wedge or a corner of three faces is one hit. At most `kMaxHitsPerStep` per step; `hit_count` counts them. `FirstHit` is the earliest hit along one straight path. | impact, swept impact |
| `hit fraction`, `hit_fraction` | No unit, 0 to 1, never above 1. When the first hit of a step happens, as a share of the step; 0 without a hit. | contact_fraction |
| `touching rule` | How a sweep treats a start whose gap is between minus one contact tolerance and 0 for the surface that gives the distance: the particle rests on that surface, which is hit only when the path pushes into it by more than one tolerance during the sweep. It belongs to solids that do not turn. After the first response of a step every solid has the stricter touched rule (`TouchedRule`), which belongs to the move, not to one solid: a start gap up to a sixteenth of the contact tolerance rests, and only an approach beyond the largest approach allowance of the responses so far is a hit. A bounding surface that does not give the distance has a rule of its own (`bounding surface`), tried first. | |
| `level with`, `ComputeIsLevelWithBoundingSurface`, `ComputeIsPassedAtBoundingSurfaceLevel` | Of a point and a bounding surface: its gap to the surface is between minus an eighth and plus a sixteenth of the contact tolerance, the gap alone, whatever the velocity. A solid that the particle passes at a bounding surface's level (level with one that does not give its distance) is not an active surface, and the sweep tries its bounding surfaces before the touching rule on its distance. Before any response, an approach to such a solid that sinks less than one tolerance in the step then gets no response, a known choice (after a response the touched rule hits it at 0); at a start within rounding of the rest gap the sign of the computed gap decides, a hair above giving a hit at 0 instead. Either way the particle never enters the solid and the settle lifts it back. | |
| `turning solid` | A solid whose shape turns during the step: a box with an angular velocity other than (0, 0, 0), a flat solid or a plate whose angular velocity has a part across its normal longer than `kNegligibleTurnShare` (2^-46) of it, the most that rounding leaves, or a cylinder whose axis tilts enough to move its surface by more than an eighth of the contact tolerance during the step. A sphere, a cylinder that only spins, and a flat solid or a plate that only spins about its normal are not turning solids. Its gap is not linear in time, so its first hit comes from the stepping search; it has no touching rule, and no bounding surface. A hit on it ends the sweeps of the step with the carry in its axes. | |
| `stepping search`, `advance`, `kMaxAdvanceCount`, `kTurningSolidHitGapShare`, `hover`, `graze` | How a sweep finds its first hit on a turning solid. From the sweep's start, each advance moves forward by the larger of the first-order advance (the gap divided by the fastest the gap can shrink) and the second-order advance, so no advance passes the moment the path reaches the solid grown by the tolerance. It stops with a hit once the gap is at most `kTurningSolidHitGapShare` (a quarter) of the tolerance, with no hit at the end of the sweep, and after `kMaxAdvanceCount` (128) advances with a close call (`kHoverNearTurningSolid`): a path that hovers near the solid or grazes it, with almost no motion along its normal, needs that many. | |
| `carry in the solid's axes`, `SolidAxesCarry`, `ApplyCarryInSolidAxes` | The rest of a step after a hit on a turning solid: the particle moves in a straight line in the solid's own axes, where the solid stands still, at the velocity relative to the surface it had at the hit, turned into those axes with the solid's axes at the middle of the time left (so a particle stuck to the solid keeps its own point and a frictionless one at rest on a face spinning about its normal stays put to third order in the step's turn a, its radius growing by a^4 / 48, +2 % at a = 1 rad), with any part into the surface removed (a part out of it is kept: on a face turning away from the particle's line the carry may end up to sin(a / 2) times the relative speed times the time left off the face, a known choice that does not build up over steps), and is placed with the solid's pose at the end of the step. The solid's distance is convex in those axes, so the particle stays at least as far from it as at the hit. The carry ends the sweeps of the step: with no touching rule, a sweep from there would hit the solid again at 0. It moves the particle, not its velocities. A particle that leaves the solid is carried too (a known choice): its free flight for the rest of the step is bent by the solid's turn, 3e-6 m off over 1 ms for a particle leaving at 3 m/s a face turning at 2 rad/s 0.2 m from its axis, and its hit records a positive approach speed (+2.6 m/s there). | |
| `closing bound` (of a carry) | m. How far any other solid can come toward the particle during a carry: the time left times the sum of the carry's speed in the solid's axes, the turning solid's linear speed and angular speed times the particle's distance from its origin, and the same two of the other solid. A solid whose gap at the hit is not beyond the closing bound makes the step too close to call (`kTurningSolidNearOtherSolid`). | |
| `fills the same space`, `ComputeIsSameSpaceThroughStep` | Of two solids: the same shape, size, pose at the start of the step and motion, bit for bit, so the same space at every time of the step; their ids, friction scales and reactions may differ. The stepping search searches only the first of them in array order, and the closing bound of a carry on one leaves the other out. | |
| `second-order advance` | The advance of the stepping search toward a solid whose distance is convex: the positive root of `g + gap_rate * s - gap_curvature_bound * s^2 / 2`, a lower bound on the gap over the advance. It is used only when it is longer than the first-order advance. | Newton step |
| `gap rate` (`gap_rate`) | m/s. How fast the gap to a solid changes at one moment: the normal times the particle's velocity minus the solid's surface velocity there. Negative while the gap closes. Papers write it with a dot over g. | |
| `gap curvature bound` (`gap_curvature_bound`) | m/s^2. The fastest the gap rate toward a turning solid can change during a sweep: the angular speed times (twice the speed relative to the solid plus the angular speed times the largest distance from the path to the solid's origin). Papers call the two terms Coriolis and centripetal. | |
| `subgradient` | Of a convex function f at a point p: a vector n with f(q) >= f(p) + n . (q - p) for every q, the slope of a plane that touches f from below at p. Where f is smooth it is the gradient; at a kink, such as the edge of a box or the middle plane of the absolute value of p.z, any slope between the two sides is one. | |
| `HasConvexDistance` | Says whether a shape's distance is convex in its own axes, with its reported normal a subgradient, which the second-order advance needs: true for the flat solid, the plate, the box, the sphere and the cylinder, false for the domain box (and a mesh), which keep the first-order advance alone. | |
| `response`, `ComputeContactResponse` | What a hit does to one velocity: the approach speed is removed, then friction reduces the sideways speed (stick or slip). | impulse (for a velocity change) |
| `approach allowance`, `approach_allowance` | m/s. What a response may have left of an approach along any active normal by rounding: `ComputeLargestApproachLeftAfterResponse` for one surface, `2^-46` of the speeds involved, the change included, for several. A move keeps the largest of its responses so far, one number per velocity, and the touched rule calls only a faster approach a hit. | |
| `approach left after the response`, `ComputeLargestApproachLeftAfterResponse` | m/s. The approach speed that rounding leaves in a velocity the response returned: zero up to a few rounding units of the speeds involved, of either sign. `ComputeLargestApproachLeftAfterResponse` bounds its size by `2^-46` of the sum of the absolute axes of the velocity and the solid velocity; a test of whether such a velocity still approaches compares with that bound, not with 0. | |
| `approach speed`, `approach_speed` | m/s. A particle's speed along a solid's normal, measured from the moving solid: `Dot(normal_out_of_solid, velocity - solid_velocity)`. Negative means approaching. A hit removes it from an approaching velocity, in world coordinates. | incoming relative normal speed |
| `sideways speed`, `sideways_speed` | m/s. A particle's speed along a solid's surface, measured from the moving solid, after the approach is removed. | tangential speed (for this) |
| `friction` | No unit, zero or more. How much sideways speed a solid takes off per unit of speed into it: the particle's `friction` times the solid's `friction_scale`. Papers: the Coulomb coefficient. | friction_coefficient |
| `stick` | At a hit, friction takes all the sideways speed: the particle takes the solid's velocity. It happens when friction is at least the sideways speed divided by the speed into the solid. | |
| `slip` | At a hit, friction takes only part of the sideways speed: the particle keeps moving along the surface, slower. | |
| `reaction`, `SolidReaction`, `reports_reaction` | The change a solid makes to a particle's velocity for the next step, which the solid needs to compute the force on it. Only a solid with `reports_reaction` (driven or free) records it. | |
| `settle` | At the end of a particle's move, one move of the particle off every surface of every solid, touched or not, that it is closer to than the contact tolerance, to the rest gap of all of them (`MoveToRestGapOfNearSurfaces`). When there is no such point the particle stays and the end check decides. | nudge |
| `slide` | The rest of the step spent moving along the surfaces a particle is on, with the velocity the joint response left: in a groove it keeps the part along the common line, in a cusp or a corner it stops, between moving solids it takes a velocity that approaches none of them. | |
| `active surface`, `ActiveSurfaces` | A surface the particle is on at a hit: of any solid, within the contact tolerance plus a sixteenth of it, with its distance and normal read at the particle's position then, or the surface the sweep hit, with the hit's normal. A solid passed at a bounding surface's level is left out unless the sweep hit it. An exact copy (same normal, solid velocity, friction and `reports_reaction`, bit for bit) counts once; the rest are sorted by value, a reacting solid before one that does not react on equal values, so the response and the reaction do not depend on the order of the solids array. A coincident surface that is not an exact copy stays: a rougher one acts for its direction (`pushing surface`), and one as rough or smoother with the same solid velocity changes nothing, or, beside a moving solid, the result by rounding only (measured: at most 2.2e-14 of the speed). Up to 16 surfaces and eight directions. | |
| `joint response`, `ApplyJointResponse` | The response of one hit over every active surface at once. One surface: the response of `ComputeContactResponse`. Several: the smallest change without friction that leaves the velocity approaching none of them, then each used surface's friction from its push, and a second change without friction when the result still approaches one or the solids move differently. Each push and friction share counts in its own solid's reaction. | |
| `direction` | Of a joint response: the active surfaces whose normals agree within 2^-40 on every axis, which the solver reads as one surface with the normal of the first of them in sorted order. | |
| `push` | m/s. Of a surface in a joint response: how far the change without friction goes along its direction's normal, the solver's multiplier for it. | impulse (for this) |
| `friction limit` | m/s. Of a surface in a joint response: friction times its `friction_scale` times its push, the most sideways speed its friction can take in that response. Surfaces with one solid velocity add their limits; surfaces moving differently apply theirs one after the other, averaged over every order, an approximation in which a surface's friction can push the particle past sticking to its own solid (measured: 5.5 % of 24,401 such generated responses, by up to 0.22 of the speeds), a known limit. | budget (for this) |
| `pushing surface` | The surface that acts for a direction in a joint response, carrying its friction and its reaction: among its surfaces that push, the one with the largest friction, then one whose solid reports a reaction, then the first in sorted order. | |
| `sliver` | Two active surfaces whose normals are within 2^-40 of opposite, a wedge under 1.35e-6 rad (a plank lying on a floor with a tilt of rounding size): the change without friction cannot be computed within the rounding allowed there, so the particle sticks to the active surface it approaches fastest. | |
| `squeeze`, `kSqueezedBetweenSolids` | A close call of the joint response: solids close on the particle so that no velocity leaves them all (a ceiling coming down on a floor), or the velocity that leaves them is faster than `kMaxSqueezeOutSpeedFactor` (64) times the speeds involved (a wedge closing at under 0.8953 degrees for one moving face, 1.7906 for two). It names the solid the failing velocity approaches fastest. The geometry and the speeds decide those: at a hit at the start of the step a shorter step does not cure them, at a later hit a step that ends before the hit does, until the solids reach the particle. The same close call is given when a response's approach allowance times the time left exceeds a sixty-fourth of the contact tolerance; a shorter step cures that one. | |
| `wedge angle` | The angle between two surfaces as the free space sees it: 180 degrees minus the angle between their normals. An acute wedge has normals more than a right angle apart (`Dot(n_A, n_B) < 0`); a flat wedge has an angle near 0. | |
| `shortfall` | m. How far a point is from a surface's rest gap: the contact tolerance minus its distance, `tol - d`, positive when the point is too near. | |
| `tangent contact` | Two convex solids touching at a point or along a line: a ball or a cylinder resting on a floor, two touching balls. The free space around the contact is a cusp. | |
| `cusp` | The free space around a tangent contact, a wedge whose angle shrinks to 0 at the contact. A particle in it rests at the rest point, the nearest point at the rest gap of both solids: `2 Sqrt(R tol)` from the contact of a ball of radius `R` resting on a floor. | |
| `rest point` | Of a wedge, a corner or a cusp: the nearest point at the rest gap of every surface there, where a particle pushed in comes to rest. | |
| `move cap`, `kMaxRestGapMoveInTolerances` | The longest move off several near surfaces, 2^22 contact tolerances: a 256th of a cell plus 4 micrometres in a one-metre scene. The particle moves without a matching velocity, so the move stays small against a cell. | |
| `move off near surfaces`, `MoveToRestGapOfNearSurfaces` | One move of a point to the rest gap of every surface it is closer to than the contact tolerance, as near to the point as the solver finds: the smallest change that meets every shortfall (`SolveSmallestCorrection`), solved again from each iterate with the distances and normals read there.
