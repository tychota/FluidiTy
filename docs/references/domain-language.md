# FluidiTy domain language

Use these terms in the specification, numerical reference, proofs, native code, and acceptance evidence. Name the translation when a concept crosses a boundary. This dictionary interprets the [design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-07-fluidity-design.md) (§§4–6, 11, 13–15) and [method paper](../papers/fluidity-method.typ); it does not change their equations, contracts, or milestone gates.

| Term | Meaning and naming consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Continuum, substance, material phase, body | A **continuum** selects governing equations and state (Liquid, Granular, Snow, Elastic, Gas); a **substance** supplies parameters; a **material phase** identifies an immiscible region; a **body** identifies a contact-connected object. The legacy 2D `MaterialPhase` enum selects a continuum for history blending. Say *stage* for an operation and *milestone* for a delivery gate. |
| MAC grid, face normal axis | A marker-and-cell (MAC) grid places pressure at cell centers and each Cartesian velocity component on the faces normal to that axis. A **face normal velocity** is a scalar in m/s. Uniform resolution and sparse storage are independent: an owned block contains a fixed number of cells at one resolution. |
| World position, lattice coordinate, slot, count, capacity, ordinal | A world position is measured in m. A lattice coordinate is a signed integer location on a cell, block, bin, or component's face lattice. Within a given uniform level and layout, the normal axis and lattice coordinate identify a face; an adaptive face identity also needs the level. A **slot** addresses storage in a particular topology version (e.g. `MacFaceStencilSample3D::face_slot`); a **count** tells how many; a **capacity** is an allocated or declared bound; an **ordinal** identifies an item in an ordered sequence. Existing `*Index3D` coordinate types are retained spellings, not array offsets. Preserve conventional coordinate/ABI names such as `Vec2::x` and `Grid2D::nx`. |
| Sort bin, tile, stencil, compatible pair | The **sort bin** is the particle's signed bin coordinate at the last sort. Its **tile** is the bounded index region checked before writing, not the owning grid block. A **stencil** is the neighborhood of weighted face samples used at a particle position. Complete support includes zero-weight sample locations and, on gather, may include ghost samples. P2G filters for structural eligibility and matching side before mass validity is known, renormalizes the compatible weights, and recenters their offsets. G2P uses the complete original basis weights and offsets. |
| P2G, deposition channels, quantum | Particle-to-grid (**P2G**) deposits physical mass (kg) and pure normal momentum (kg·m/s) in branches that carry physical mass, with a separate stress force (N) where applicable. Gas transport markers deposit numerical quadrature weights. Grid thermodynamics supplies physical gas density and inertia. Identify a single particle's `stress_force_contribution`, its `scaled_stress_force_contribution` in quanta, the rounded integer `quantized_stress_force_contribution`, and the face's integer `stress_force_quanta_sum` separately. The scale is the *deposited quantity per quantum*. Fixed-point deposition guards each scaled contribution, rounds to nearest with ties to even, and then sums the integers. A reserved zero liquid channel is not evidence of particle stress. |
| G2P, PIC, velocity-fit slopes, velocity gradient | Grid-to-particle (**G2P**) gathers a particle-in-cell (**PIC**) velocity and, in affine particle-in-cell (**APIC**), velocity-fit slopes `C`. A row gives the fitted spatial variation of one velocity component; `L` is the derivative of the interpolated velocity from weight gradients. Both are in s⁻¹, but are generally different. Rows of `C` and `L` are indexed by velocity component. A pressure row is one cell's pressure equation. |
| Pre-force snapshot, projected velocity, grid increment, history residual | The **pre-force snapshot** `v_old` is the normalized, blended face field at `tⁿ`. The **predicted** velocity `u*` (`predicted_normal_velocity`) enters pressure assembly and correction; the **projected** field `u_new` is at `tⁿ + dt`. Gather both complete fields at the same frozen particle position, with separate ghost samples and matching wall times. **Grid velocity increment** is new PIC minus old PIC; **particle history residual** (`history_velocity_residual` or `particle_minus_old_pic_velocity`) is the prior particle velocity minus old PIC. The history blend uses the residual. Name stored particle velocity and advection velocity separately. |
| Simulation interface, signed distance, aperture, open face area | Simulate owns interface geometry; render reconstruction consumes published data. Liquid signed distance is negative inside, the normal points out, and curvature of a sphere is positive. **Aperture** is a fraction; **open face area** is a physical area in m². **Particle volume ratio** `J_p` (`volume_ratio` in native particle state) is dimensionless; **pressure jump** is in Pa. Do not use a common code name for the two quantities denoted by `J` in equations. |
| Pressure solve, gauge, velocity correction, flux, defect | The solve determines cell pressure (Pa); the gauge removes the constant null mode of an unanchored component, and velocity correction applies the pressure to normal face velocities. **Flux** is a signed volume rate (m³/s). The **original pressure system** retains all cell equations before gauge restriction. Its **integrated residual** is `b − Ap`. A cell's **physical integrated defect** is corrected outward fluid flux plus outward blocked-area wall flux, minus the integrated source, plus the compliance term `C_i(p_i − p_i*)`, with `p_i*` the previous pressure. The defect equals the negative residual when evaluated with the same frozen links; division by positive cell fluid volume gives a local rate in s⁻¹. |
| Coarse–fine transition, particle narrow band | A transition joins resolutions; the narrow band specifies where particles carry the model near an interface. A 2D transition fixture is separate evidence from 3D edge/corner support, flux, and cost. |
| Trial state, committed state, checkpoint, committed point frame | **Committed A** is the immutable accepted state and clock. **Trial B** holds proposed persistent state under its own topology; **scratch** holds temporary or derived data. Acceptance installs the whole trial and advances the clock once; rejection leaves A intact. The immutable **frame-start snapshot** supports capacity replay. An **asynchronous host snapshot** carries an epoch; the runtime fences its source buffers until the copy completes. A **checkpoint** carries restart state; a **committed point frame** carries published point data at a frame time. Use **point cache** for playback/render input, **checkpoint store** for restart, and **Blender timeline frame** for the UI time index. A point-cache reader maps published samples to Blender. A Blender **preview bake** runs a short solve under its own cache identity. Native CLI **--preview** is read-only with respect to published output. Cache playback reads existing frames. |
| CUDA Graph executable, replay, stream interval | The executable is the instantiated enqueue sequence. A **CUDA Graph replay** submits it again. **Checkpoint replay** reruns a solver interval from a checkpoint; **Resume** is the user action that continues an interrupted bake and can use checkpoint replay. Separate live particle count from particle slot capacity. CUDA event timing for a queued sequence is its **stream interval**; host submission and capture/instantiation are separate measurements. |
| Reference, proof, probe, qualification | Lean proves exact statements under named hypotheses. Independent Python or analytic fixtures give numerical oracles. Native CPU/CUDA parity, runtime, and reproducibility checks assess the implementation. Target end-to-end runs measure full-job quality and performance. Report these four kinds of evidence separately. A **probe** checks a specific capability; a **feasibility study** evaluates a candidate; **qualification** applies pass/fail gates to a pinned configuration. A passing check describes a run, and exact arithmetic is separate evidence from runtime or GPU behavior. |

Read the [reference conventions](../../reference/README.md), [proof scope and runtime bridge](../../proofs/README.md), and native layouts in [MAC types](../../native/include/fluidity/base/mac_types_3d.h), [transfer types](../../native/include/fluidity/base/mac_transfer_types_3d.h), and [boundary transfer types](../../native/include/fluidity/base/liquid_boundary_transfer_types_3d.h) for details.

Apply seven checks to each name: **consistency** (same concept, same term), **understandability** (recognizable meaning), **specificity** (role, frame, units, or time level as needed), **brevity** (only useful words), **searchability** (one recognizable spelling), **pronounceability** (usable in review), and **austerity** (literal, durable wording). Keep standard terms, coordinate symbols, and wire names where their contracts require them.
